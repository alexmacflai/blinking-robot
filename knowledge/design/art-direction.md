# Art direction

`[design]` `[art-direction]` `[scene]`

## Core stance

Blinking Robot images are quiet, strange, graphic, and understated. They feel
observed rather than staged: an impossible or awkward thing is presented
matter-of-factly, without an explanation or a demand for attention. Each image
should have one clear visual idea and may leave some things unresolved.

## Decision principles

- Simplify before adding.
- Prioritise mood and composition over correctness.
- Prefer one strong idea over several clever ones.
- Break realism when it improves the image.
- Leave some things unresolved.

## Composition and hierarchy

- Build with large, simple masses and strong negative space.
- Keep focal points few. A subject may be small in frame when that makes the
  composition more interesting; do not fill the canvas merely because space
  exists.
- Think poster, still, or frame—not a concept-art scene.
- Decide what deserves information. Everything else may collapse into shape or
  texture; do not distribute detail evenly.
- A background may be almost abstract. Recognition threshold is enough.

## Form and physics

- Reduce objects to recognisable essentials. Crude, simple shapes are better
  than descriptive ones, and silhouette matters more than surface detail.
- Avoid unnecessary internal detail. Forms may merge, disappear, or fragment.
- Impossible things should remain ordinary. Physics, scale, depth, and material
  behaviour may be ambiguous or bend without becoming fantasy.

## Tone

- Be deadpan: quiet, restrained, slightly detached, dry, subdued, and odd.
- Let humour arise from contradiction, timing, scale, awkwardness, or
  literalisation—not gags.
- Melancholy is welcome when it is not melodramatic. Prefer emotional ambiguity
  and a slightly unresolved or anticlimactic feeling over a single obvious mood.
- Vulnerability should come from isolation, emptiness, repetition, awkward
  movement, or smallness, rather than overt symbolism.

## Avoid

- Generic AI concept art, over-rendering, and gratuitous detail.
- Fantasy grandeur, spectacle, polish, or decorative clutter.
- Epic, cute, whimsical, sentimental, magical, or inspirational framing.
- “Cute retro pixel art” unless a specific brief explicitly calls for it.
- Realism for realism's sake.
